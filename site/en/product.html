Rubrics and scorecards

The same questions for everyone.
Scores you can compare.

A rubric is a set of questions with weights and a description of what a 1 and a 5 look like. Every interviewer gets the same one, scores independently, and the stage score calculates itself. No gut feelings.

Rubric editor

Question, weight, time — and a description for every score

You start from a template, or ask AI for questions based on the job description. Every question has anchors: what an answer needs to include to earn a 3, and what earns a 5.

Scorecard

Interviewers see the questions, the weights and their own scorecard. Nothing else.

Other people's scores appear only after you submit yours. That way the first opinion doesn't pull the rest along, and disagreements stay real instead of getting smoothed over.

  • 1–5 scale with a description for every question
  • AI suggests a score with a quote; you decide
  • Stage score weighted by question weights

Disagreements

A 2 and a 5 on the same question? Listen to the clip before you decide.

When interviewers differ by 2 points or more, Tekma flags the question and links to the moment in the recording. You discuss evidence, not impressions.

  • Flag on the scorecard, the kanban board and the dashboard
  • Calibration: who scores more leniently, who more strictly
  • No decision until two scores are in

Why it works

Four things that improve the quality of your decisions

01

Same questions

Every candidate for the same role answers an identical set. You compare answers, not conversations.

Learn more →
02

Weights, not impressions

A system design question weighs ×3, code quality ×1. The stage score comes from the weights, not from a “general feeling.”

Learn more →
03

Independent scores

An interviewer can't see anyone else's scores until they submit their own. The first opinion doesn't set the tone for the rest.

Learn more →
04

Disagreement is a signal

A gap of 2+ points gets a flag and a link to the recording. That's usually where the best decision is.
