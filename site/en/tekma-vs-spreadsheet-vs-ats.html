Tekma vs spreadsheet vs ATS: what should you run hiring on?

You can run hiring in a spreadsheet, in a classic applicant tracking system (ATS) or in a tool built around evaluating candidates. We compare them fairly: what each does well, what it does not do, and when to pick which.

What to choose

Three situations, three different answers

There is no single best option. It depends on how many people you hire, how many interviewers evaluate them, and what costs you the most time.

Spreadsheet

A spreadsheet is enough if…

  • you hire a few people a year,
  • one or two people interview candidates,
  • you do not need interview transcripts,
  • you have time to keep things tidy and delete data after each search.

Keep the rubric on its own tab, with one score column per interviewer. We describe how to set up the criteria in our guide to the interview scorecard.

Classic ATS

An ATS is the better fit if…

  • you run many searches at once or hire in volume,
  • you post jobs to many boards with one click,
  • you have a team of recruiters and multi-step approvals,
  • you need detailed reporting for a recruiting team.

If that is you, check whether the ATS you pick has structured scorecards and independent scoring, because that is a common gap.

Tekma

Tekma makes sense if…

  • you care about the quality of decisions: the same questions, independent scores, evidence from interviews,
  • you want hiring and the first 90 days in one place,
  • you use AI in hiring and need human oversight and a decision log,
  • you hire for a team of 10 to 300 people, without a large HR department.

Already have an ATS? You can import candidates and stage history from Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby or a CSV file.

Questions

Common questions about choosing a tool

Does Tekma replace an ATS?

It depends on what you need from an ATS. Tekma covers a candidate pipeline board, rubrics and scorecards, a notetaker, a candidate portal, a careers page and a first-90-days plan. If your main job is posting to many job boards at once or building detailed reports for a recruiting team, check whether that scope is enough for you. If you already have an ATS, you can import candidates and stage history from it.

Can I start with a spreadsheet and move to Tekma later?

Yes. Tekma accepts candidate and stage-history imports from a CSV file. To make the move easier, keep one column per rubric criterion in your spreadsheet and a separate tab for each interviewer.

How much does Tekma cost?

The Free plan costs €0 and includes 1 open role and 5 AI-analyzed interviews per month, along with rubrics, the notetaker, scorecards, a careers page and the candidate portal. The Pro plan costs €129 per month and includes unlimited roles and 60 AI-analyzed interviews per month, then €2 per interview. Scale is priced individually. You can add teammates at no charge, and candidates always use the portal for free.

Can hiring in a spreadsheet be GDPR-compliant?

It can, but then compliance rests entirely on you: informing candidates, restricting access to the file and deleting data once the process ends. UODO, Poland's data protection authority, states that data of candidates you did not hire should generally be deleted promptly after the process ends, unless the candidate agreed to longer storage. That is hard to enforce in spreadsheets passed around by email.

Does the AI in Tekma make decisions about candidates?

No. The AI proposes a score with a quote from the interview and shows the resume match with an explanation, but it never rejects or moves candidates. A person makes the decision, after two independent scores.
